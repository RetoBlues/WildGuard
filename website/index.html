Wildlife detection & deterrence · Charlottesville, Virginia

WildGuard

Smart Protection, Wild Solutions.

Solar-powered sensor nodes that detect deer with 24 GHz radar and a backup PIR sensor, then turn them away with randomized light and sound. One platform, two products: a roadside system that lights up regulated warning signs for drivers, and a compact garden sensor homeowners control from an app.

Exhibit 1 · Market segmentation & targeting

Three buyers, one problem

Deer cost Virginia drivers, road crews and homeowners every year. We start where the cost is clearest and the buyer is a single decision-maker.

1

VDOT & municipalities

8.6M

registered vehicles and 6.1M licensed drivers in Virginia (2024), growing about 1.17% a year

VDOT recorded 7,335 deer-related crashes and 690 injuries in 2023 and spends heavily tracking and clearing carcasses. VDOT spends an estimated $4–5M a year cleaning up after deer collisions. Fewer crashes means fewer taxpayer dollars spent on cleanup.

How we sized the market

  1. Garden: 3,779,544 Virginia households (Census, July 2025) × 69.6% owner-occupied × 55% who garden × 73% outside dense cities = 1,056,171 potential buyers in Year 1.
  2. Roadside: 9,801 HOAs × 60% in high-deer-density counties × 40% near wooded edges, plus 73 DCR-designated collision zones = 2,548 potential sites.
  3. Growth: 7.5% a year for garden (electronic animal-repellent CAGR, DataHorizon Research); 1.5% for roadside (national HOA growth).
  4. Share: garden rises 5% → 25% by Year 5, toward the 25.4% share eco-friendly products hold (NYU Stern). Roadside holds a conservative 5%, since HOA vendors turn over often, budgets are set once a year, and an installed site does not buy again. We also expect to partner with VDOT and DWR on designated wildlife corridors.

Seasonality matters: about 1 in 5 deer accidents happen between October and December, which is also when HOAs review budgets.

Exhibit 3 · Perceptual maps & positioning

Where we sit against competitors

Garden sensor

Positioning: we measure effectiveness over six months and longer. Competing products draw complaints about breaking or failing within weeks. WildGuard layers radar and PIR detection with 100+ sound and light patterns so deer do not get used to it, all cruelty-free.

Roadside sensor

Positioning: effective roadside detection systems typically sell for around $500,000. WildGuard's system of 16 or more sensors and 2 signs sells for about $25,000, which puts collision prevention within reach of HOAs and smaller municipalities.

Exhibit 4 · Marketing mix

Product, price, place, promotion

Family branding under one name, so both products build the same reputation for reliable, humane protection. The detection-and-deterrence market is in the growth stage of its life cycle.

Product

Two products, one shared board and bill of materials. The deer logo and the slogan “Smart Protection, Wild Solutions” leave room to add more wildlife products later.

Price

Garden: competitive and value-based, $125 held flat for all five years, on about $46 of parts. Roadside: about $25,000 per system. An average site needs about 40 nodes, roughly $5,000 of materials, plus substantial installation labor.

Place

Selective multichannel. Garden sells on our website, Amazon, Home Depot and Lowe's, shipped daily by UPS Ground. Roadside sells direct B2B to HOAs, municipalities and transportation departments. Support runs online, with on-site help for large accounts.

Promotion

One message: proactive, humane protection that works. Spend runs steadily and peaks in spring and fall, when deer activity is highest. Free pilot installs let HOAs and VDOT districts see it work first. In 2029 a $1.0M contest installs roadside systems in 40 communities at no charge.

Promotion budget by year

Integrated marketing communications, $ thousands. 2029 jumps to $3.0M to fund the $1.0M community pilot.

Sales force

Salespeople by year. $30k base plus 4% commission on individual sales, managed against monthly and quarterly goals for revenue, conversion, new accounts and retention.

Exhibit 8 · Quality assurance

A warning that doesn't fire is worse than none

We run Total Quality Management: test records, complaints, false alerts, missed detections and repair history feed back into equipment choices, calibration guidelines and the technician checklist. Operations compete on quality and consistency, responsiveness, and speed: every unit is tested and calibrated before handoff, and every complaint gets a response within one business day.

Reliability

Detection, light activation and power are tested before installation and again on site, and the results are recorded.

Performance

Each sensor is positioned and calibrated for its road and terrain, with test passes before the customer accepts it.

Durability

Components are inspected, connections sealed and weatherproofed, and every installation documented.

Serviceability

System status is monitored, faults are logged, and repairs are retested on a set schedule.

Proactive

An installation checklist and a record for every site: equipment, settings, test results and maintenance history. Technicians train on the checklist and review recurring faults so later installs improve.

Recovery plan

A missed detection, false alert or failure is logged and acknowledged within one business day. A technician inspects, repairs or replaces parts under the customer agreement, recalibrates, and repeats the on-site tests. The customer hears what happened, what was fixed and when it passed. Repeat issues trigger a review of the equipment or procedure.

Risk analysis · Porter's five forces

What could go wrong, and our answer

Competitive rivalry

Pressure: established brands such as Orbit, Hoont and CrossTek.

Our answer: a roadside system at about $25K against roughly $500K for rival systems.

New entrants

Pressure: sensor devices are easy to copy.

Our answer: public contracts, MUTCD standards and a patent filing raise the barrier.

Substitutes

Pressure: cheaper fencing, sprays and static deer signs.

Our answer: WildGuard detects and reacts in real time, and a roadside system pays for itself after a few prevented crashes.

Buyer power

Pressure: customers compare prices and work with tight budgets; HOAs and municipalities cut project spending when rates are high.

Our answer: brand loyalty and ongoing software updates.

Supplier power

Pressure: key electronics come from China and face tariff risk.

Our answer: off-the-shelf parts with U.S. backup suppliers such as Digi-Key and Polycase.

People & IP

Pressure: losing a key specialist would slow production, and an unpatentable design would invite larger copycats.

Our answer: yearly raises and clear promotion paths, plus a $30K patent and trademark budget in Year 1.
